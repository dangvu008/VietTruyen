The Whispering Woods

Chapter Twelve

The air beneath the canopy of the Whispering Woods felt thick, as if the oxygen had been replaced by the weight of a thousand unspoken memories. Elara stepped over a gnarled root that pulsed with a faint, rhythmic bioluminescence. She didn't belong here, and the forest knew it. Every snap of a twig under her leather boots echoed like a gunshot in the oppressive silence.

"I was told you were watching," Elara whispered, her hand instinctively drifting to the silver pendant tucked beneath her tunic. The metal felt ice-cold against her skin, a stark contrast to the humid warmth of the grove.

Suddenly, the fog began to churn. It didn't drift with the wind; it spiraled, coiling like a serpent around the base of a monolithic oak. From the heart of the mist, a shape emerged—tall, ethereal, and flickering like a dying candle. The Ancient Spirit did not have a face, only a mask of woven bark and two points of starlight where eyes should have been.

"The daughter of the Weaver returns," the Spirit’s voice was not a sound, but a vibration that resonated in Elara’s marrow. "You carry the weight of your father’s debt, yet you walk with the stride of a free woman. Why?"

Elara drew a shaky breath, forcing herself not to look away from those piercing starlight eyes. "I walk to settle that debt, once and for all. Or to burn the ledger entirely."
